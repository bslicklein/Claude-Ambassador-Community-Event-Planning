# The decision report

The host has to approve or reject hundreds of people and will not read a CSV to do it.
The report is what they actually read, and it is where the research gets defended.

Write it as a self-contained HTML file to the event folder and `open` it. Claude
branding, per `~/CLAUDE.md`: `#FAF9F5` ground, `#1F1E1D` text, `#D97757` accent, serif
headings, ~760px column. Every diagram inline SVG, with a one-line caption above and a
one-sentence "what to notice" below it.

## Sections, in order

**1. TL;DR callout.** The single most important finding, not a summary of the process.
The one that mattered: *the registration form systematically hides your best people, so
a ranking built on the CSV alone would have buried them* — followed immediately by
three named examples.

**2. Stat tile row.** Total registrants · already approved · ranked · deep-researched ·
upgraded on the re-run · community leaders found · real audiences found · tables.

**3. The pipeline diagram.** Four boxes: registrants → structured pass (all of them) →
deep research (N profiles, M agents) → composite score → full ranking. Caption what the
research stage actually changed in rank positions.

**4. How the score is built.** Horizontal stacked bar, one segment per component,
labelled with points and what feeds it. The "what to notice" line must state the
deliberate trade: influence 40 against Claude Code depth 10, and why.

**5. The founder check.** Three-branch flow diagram. State explicitly that demotion
only fires on a confident negative.

**6. Research coverage, before and after the re-run.** Two confidence bars side by side.
Name what the re-run rescued, what false negative it reversed, and what mistaken-identity
match it caught. This section is the audit trail for anyone who asks "how do you know?"

**7. The people the form hid.** A table: rank · name · what they wrote · what they
actually are · evidence. This is the most persuasive section in the document and the
strongest argument for keeping research as a standing step.

**8. Community operators, invite these personally.** The cross-promotion list. These
people get a personal note from the host, not a Luma template.

**9. The room, against the brief.** The host asked for a specific mix of personas and
industries. Show asked, seated, and available-in-the-pool side by side, as a grouped
bar, then seniority, industries, audience sizes, and the brand list spelled out in
full. Show the whole-pool distribution next to the invited one so the effect of the
floors and caps is visible. Where a floor could not be filled, say it here in the
positive: this is what the pool could supply, and here is who you would have to recruit
directly to close the gap.

**10. Workshop tables.** The three-rule diagram, the "why not sort by skill" panel, then
every table listed with its people, industry, role band, builder count, and each
person's one-line note.

**11. Flags before you send anything.** Direct competitors in the pool. Self-declared
founders with no discoverable company. Anyone approved with no research behind them.
Every persona or industry floor the pool could not fill. This section exists so the host makes the judgment calls, not the
model, and it goes last because it is the final gate before the first email leaves.

## Rules

- Never publish the report as a shareable artifact. It contains verdicts on named
  private individuals, several unflattering. It is a local working file for the host.
- Quote what someone wrote on the form verbatim, and separate it clearly from what
  research found. Never blend a claim and a finding into one sentence.
- Where research could not confirm something, the report says "could not confirm",
  never "does not exist".
- Read the flags section aloud to the host before any status change is pushed.
