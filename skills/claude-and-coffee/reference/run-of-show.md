# Run of show

The shape that works for a 5:00 to 8:00 drop-in Claude & Coffee, 100 seats.

| Time | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| −90 min | Host arrives. Board on the easel at the door, banner beside it, badges alphabetised on the check-in table, drink tickets counted out | Host |
| −45 min | Venue walkthrough: wifi tested on a real phone, sound tested from the back of the room, table numbers placed | Host + venue |
| 5:00 | Doors. Badge, drink ticket, point at their table number | Greeter |
| 5:30 | Keynote, short. Fifteen minutes is plenty | Host |
| 6:15 | Coworking at tables. Host circulates rather than sits | Everyone |
| 7:30 | Last call, feedback QR on screen and on the board | Host |
| 8:00 | Close. **Settle the tab and ask for the itemized invoice before leaving** | Host |

## Before you leave the venue

- Settle the drink tab and get the receipt in writing, not verbally.
- Ask for the itemized invoice with space rental separated from food and beverage.
  If it is not in hand, get a name and a date for when it will be.
- Photograph the room while it is still full. The board next to the easel is the
  best single documentation shot, and the recap post needs it.
- Count leftover badges. That number is your no-show rate, and it is the input to
  how many seats you over-approve next time.

## The greeter table

One person who is not the host owns the door for the first forty-five minutes.
The host cannot both welcome people and run check-in, and the check-in table is
where the room's first impression is made.

Badges are alphabetised by **first** name, because that is what people tell you.
Keep the blanks and a marker within reach; walk-ups are 10 to 15% and they are
often the people you most want back.

## What to bring that nobody remembers

Easel (venues say they have one and then do not), marker, blank badges, tape,
a phone charger, the drink tickets, and a printed copy of the table assignments
in case a phone dies.
