# Deck arc

Every Claude Community event deck is a chapter frame wrapped around a keynote.
The frame is the community's standing introduction and barely changes between
events. The keynote is the only genuinely new writing, and it is where the
effort goes.

## The chapter frame

Nine slides. Copy them forward from the last deck and edit the specifics.

| # | Slide | What changes each event |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Deck title, event name, date, venue, the icon in the arch |
| 2 | Welcome | Which ambassador is presenting, and their vertical |
| 3 | Why we're here | Nothing, unless the audience is unusual |
| 4 | What the community is | Ambassador count and any new format |
| 5 | Ambassadors | Add or remove an ambassador, update a vertical |
| 6 | Industries | Reorder so the room's dominant industry reads first |
| 7 | Formats | The "you are at one" line moves to tonight's format |
| 8 | Housekeeping timeline | Every time. Real times, real venue logistics |
| 9 | Badges or seating | Only if the event uses badges or assigned tables |

Slides 3 through 7 are effectively fixed copy. Rewriting them each event costs
time and loses the repetition that makes returning attendees feel oriented.

## The keynote

Three parts, each opened by a clay section divider. Roughly twenty to
twenty-five slides total, thirty minutes spoken.

**Part One · the reframe.** Name the belief the room already holds, then show
why it is a ceiling. The pattern order that worked: section divider → big quote
of the wrong belief → the reframe with stacked bars → the comparison table →
one figure proving the difference compounds.

**Part Two · the mechanics.** The teachable content. Start with a definitions
table so the vocabulary is settled, then a flow chain showing how the pieces
connect, then one slide per piece. Each piece gets a concept slide (what it is)
and, where it earns it, a product screenshot (where it lives in the UI) or a
quality-lever slide (what changes when you have it). Close the part with a
composition slide showing all the pieces running as one system, and a process
matrix proving it generalises past the example.

**Part Three · the bar.** Volume without quality is a downgrade, so the last
part is how you keep the bar. Definition, graders, evaluations. Short.

## The landing

| Slide | Job |
|---|---|
| Checklist | The photograph slide. Questions they can ask themselves tomorrow |
| Homework | One task, sized to thirty minutes, this week |
| Close | The title said back as a full sentence, on clay |
| CTA and QR | Feedback QR, the site paths, the community link |

## Rhythm rules

- A clay slide roughly every four slides. Section dividers do most of that work.
- Never two figure-heavy slides back to back. Put a text slide between them.
- Never two screenshots back to back unless they are in the same split slide.
- The audience should be able to name what changed on every slide within two
  seconds of it appearing. If they cannot, the slide is doing two jobs.

## Sizing

| Event format | Deck length | Spoken |
|---|---|---|
| Meetup or Claude & Coffee | 30 to 36 slides | 30 minutes, then breakouts |
| Conversation | Frame plus 6 to 8 | 10 minutes, then discussion |
| Workshop | Frame plus 10 to 12 | 20 minutes, then hands on keyboards |
| Impact Lab | Frame plus 8, heavy on the brief | 15 minutes, then build |

For the shorter formats, cut from Part Two first. It is the part that survives
being reduced to a definitions table and one flow chain.
